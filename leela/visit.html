Staying at Leela

Getting here

Up the road from Lyons.

40.22° N · 105.27° W · about 5,400 ft

Leela sits in the foothills just outside Lyons, Colorado, where the plains turn into mountains. It's about 25 minutes from Boulder, an hour from Denver, and roughly 75 minutes from Denver International Airport.

The road

The last stretch is gravel. Most cars are fine. In snow, you'll want all-wheel drive or good tires. We post road conditions in winter.

Sharing rides

We keep a ride board for programs and retreats. Fewer cars, more conversations.

What to bring

Layers. Then more layers.

Weather in the foothills changes quickly. A warm afternoon can turn into a cold evening in an hour, and summer storms roll in most afternoons.

  • A warm layer and a rain shell, in any season
  • Shoes you can walk on uneven ground in
  • A water bottle. The air up here is dry.
  • A headlamp if you're staying past dark
  • A book, a sketchbook, or nothing at all

How we live here

A few customs.

Phones in the basket

The practice room, library, and dinner table are screen-free, and many gatherings are phone-free. There's a basket by the House door. Your phone will be fine.

Clear-minded

Leela is a substance-free property. Please leave alcohol and recreational drugs at home.

Quiet hours

From 9:30pm to 7am the land is quiet. Some retreats keep silence all day. You'll see small signs when they do.

Leave it better

Wash your cup. Pick up the stray piece of trash. Stack a few logs if the pile is low. Everyone helps take care of the place a little.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Do I need to be Buddhist, or spiritual at all?

No. Buddhist practice is an important influence here, and many of our meditation teachers come from the dharma. But Leela belongs to no single tradition, and nobody will ask what you believe. Plenty of people come just for the quiet, the food, and the land.

I've never meditated. Can I come to morning practice?

Yes. Come a few minutes early and someone will show you where to sit and what to do, which is less than you'd think. Chairs are always available.

Can I bring my dog?

Dogs are welcome in designated areas and at some events. They're marked on the calendar. Please keep dogs leashed, out of the House and practice room, and away from the garden. Leela has a resident dog who will likely come to say hello.

Is Leela for kids and families?

Leela is mostly for adults, but we host family days and family-friendly programs through the year. Look for them on the calendar. Kids are always welcome at those, and at seasonal celebrations like the equinox and solstice fires.

Can I drink or bring cannabis?

Leela is a clear-minded, substance-free place, so no alcohol or recreational drugs on the property, please. It makes for quieter nights and easier mornings. Prescribed medications are your own business.

Will I have cell service?

Sometimes. Coverage on the land is patchy and we like it that way. For emergencies there's a landline in the House, and staff can always be reached.

I can't afford a retreat. Is there a way to come?

Yes. Morning practice and many gatherings are free. Most workshops are sliding scale. Retreats have scholarships, and work-trade is another way to stay. Leela's paid programs exist partly so these can exist.

How access works

How rustic is it, really?

Rustic in the good ways. The beds are good, the lamps are warm, and the food is made with care. The paths are gravel, bathrooms are a short walk from the yurts, and the weather gets a say in most plans.

Is the land accessible?

The House is reachable from the parking area on a firm, level path. Much of the rest of the land is uneven, hilly terrain. Tell us what you need before you come and we'll do our best to make it work.

Can I just come and do nothing?

Please do. It's one of the things Leela is for.
